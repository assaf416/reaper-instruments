## Usage Tips

- **Leads (Mono)**: Presets 34, 36, 38, 39, 41, 42, 44, 46, 47, 49
  - Use **Glide** for portamento between notes (smooth transitions in melodies)
  - Adjust **LFO to Pitch** for vibrato expression after note onset
  - Use **Mod Wheel (CC1)** to add vibrato dynamically during performance

- **Pads (Polyphonic)**: Presets 35, 37, 40, 43, 45, 48
  - Layer multiple pads together (this synth is polyphonic, so play chords)
  - **Sustain Pedal (CC64)** extends release time for dramatic effect
  - Adjust **Reverb Mix** to create depth and space

- **Effects Stack**:
  - **Chorus**: Creates richness in pads, adds motion to static sounds
  - **Delay**: Adds rhythmic dimension to leads, creates echo for atmosphere
  - **Reverb**: Essential for all pads and atmospheric sounds
  - **Drive**: Use sparingly on leads for bite; use on bass presets for grit

- **Sound Design Tips**:
  - Lower **Cutoff** for darker, more aggressive sounds (especially Emerson presets)
  - Increase **Resonance** for that classic Moog filter sweep
  - Use **Key Tracking** to track the keyboard and keep brightness across octaves
  - Adjust **Unison Voices** and **Spread** for thicker, wider pads

## Inspiration Sources

These presets capture the essence of classic analog synthesizer voices:

- **Rick Wakeman's YES era** (1971-1974): technically brilliant, harmonically dense keyboard textures
- **Keith Emerson's ELP classics** (1970-1975): virtuosic, theatrical, innovative synth work with heavy modulation
- **Pink Floyd's cosmic soundscapes** (1973-1977): ethereal, washy effects-laden synth pads and solos
- **Duran Duran's synth-pop perfection** (1978-1986): bright, cutting synth leads and atmospheric pads
- **Gary Numan's electronic pioneer** (1979): iconic pulse-based bass and synth-pop aesthetics
- **The Human League's synth-pop** (1986): punchy, rhythmic synth pads and percussive leads
- **Genesis' progressive synth work** (1976-1991): from Peter Gabriel's atmospheric work to Phil Collins' funky bass

Each preset is a starting point for your own explorations of these legendary tones.
